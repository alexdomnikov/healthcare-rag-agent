# Ablation Studies

All ablations use the 18 vector-search document questions in `eval/ground_truth.json`. Generated by `eval/ablations/run_all.py`. Do not edit by hand.

---

## Ablation 1: retrieval mode (hybrid vs dense vs lexical)

**Question:** Does combining BM25 and dense retrieval outperform either method alone?

**Setup:** `do_rerank=False`, `top_k=10`, `strategy='hybrid_chunker'`.
Reranker disabled so the delta reflects only first-pass retrieval quality.

| Mode    | Recall@1 | Recall@3 | Recall@5 | Recall@10 | MRR   |
|---------|----------|----------|----------|-----------|-------|
| hybrid  | 0.333    | 0.611    | 0.611    | 0.611     | 0.463 |
| vector  | 0.333    | 0.556    | 0.556    | 0.556     | 0.435 |
| lexical | 0.056    | 0.056    | 0.056    | 0.056     | 0.056 |

**Interpretation:** Hybrid wins on Recall@5 (0.611), ahead of dense-only by +0.056 points and lexical-only by +0.556 points. MRR winner is hybrid (0.463). The gap between hybrid and dense-only shows that the CMS corpus contains exact-match signals (CFR section codes, specific dollar figures) that BM25 captures and the embedding model misses. The gap between hybrid and lexical-only shows that paraphrase-resistant questions require semantic retrieval to surface.

## Ablation 2 — Reranker: on vs off

**Question:** Does cross-encoder reranking improve Recall@5 over raw hybrid retrieval?

**Setup:** hybrid mode, `strategy='hybrid_chunker'`, `top_k=5`. Reranker-on fetches
50 first-pass candidates then reranks; reranker-off returns the raw hybrid top 5.

| Condition    | Recall@1 | Recall@3 | Recall@5 | MRR   |
|--------------|----------|----------|----------|-------|
| reranker off | 0.333    | 0.611    | 0.611    | 0.463 |
| reranker on  | 0.500    | 0.833    | 0.833    | 0.657 |

**Interpretation:** Reranking improves Recall@5 by +0.222 and MRR by +0.194. The cross-encoder rescores 50 first-pass candidates rather than just the top-5, surfacing chunks that hybrid ranked between 6th and 50th. The hybrid stage is strong at recall but mediocre at precision; the cross-encoder corrects that.

## Ablation 3 — Chunking strategy: structure-aware vs fixed-size

**Question:** Does structure-aware (Docling HybridChunker) chunking outperform naive
fixed-size 512-token chunking with 50-token overlap?

**Setup:** hybrid mode, `do_rerank=False`, `top_k=10` in both conditions.

| Strategy        | Recall@1 | Recall@3 | Recall@5 | Recall@10 | MRR   |
|-----------------|----------|----------|----------|-----------|-------|
| structure-aware | 0.333    | 0.611    | 0.611    | 0.611     | 0.463 |
| fixed-size 512  | 0.389    | 0.611    | 0.667    | 0.778     | 0.518 |

**Interpretation:** Fixed-size chunking outperforms structure-aware on Recall@5 by +0.056 and MRR by +0.055. The structure-aware chunks were built with CHUNK_MAX_TOKENS=400, producing smaller chunks than the 512-token fixed windows. On this corpus the larger windows capture more relevant context per chunk. Structure-aware is still used in production for its section_path metadata and cleaner citations.

## Production config

Based on all three ablations, the locked-in defaults are:

| Setting | Value | Rationale |
|---------|-------|-----------|
| Retrieval mode | `hybrid` | Highest Recall@5 in Ablation 1 |
| Reranker | `on` | Improves Recall@5 in Ablation 2 |
| Chunking strategy | `fixed-size` | Wins in Ablation 3 |

These defaults are encoded in `src/healthcare_rag/config.py`.
